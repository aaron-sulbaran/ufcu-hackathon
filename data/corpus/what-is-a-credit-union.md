A credit union is a not-for-profit financial cooperative owned by the people it serves. A bank is owned by outside shareholders. That is the whole difference, and everything else follows from it.

Members elect volunteer board directors, and every member gets an equal vote regardless of how much money is in the account. Earnings go back into the credit union: better services, member education, lower loan rates, fewer fees, and dividends on savings, instead of payouts to outside shareholders.

Deposits are federally insured by the NCUA up to $250,000, the credit union equivalent of FDIC insurance at a bank.

The article is honest about the tradeoffs. Membership has eligibility requirements, a credit union may have fewer physical branches than a national bank, and loan approval still depends on your individual credit.

UFCU was founded in 1936 and serves Texas communities through Austin-area branches, online and mobile banking, and access to more than 55,000 fee-free ATMs nationwide.

If you are moving over from a large bank, this is the plainest answer to what changes: ownership, and who the profit is for.
