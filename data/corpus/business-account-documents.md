A business account attaches to a personal membership, so the personal Savings account comes first. What the business itself needs depends on the entity type.

A sole proprietorship brings an Assumed Name Certificate, the DBA, if the owner's first and last name is not in the business name, plus an SSN or EIN. An LLC brings the Certificate of Formation filed with the Texas Secretary of State plus an EIN, though a single-member LLC may use an SSN instead. A PLLC brings the same plus proof of Texas professional licensing. A corporation, for-profit or not-for-profit, brings the Certificate of Formation and an EIN. A general partnership brings the partnership agreement naming the business and partners, an Assumed Name Certificate, and an EIN. A limited partnership brings the Certificate of Formation and an EIN. An unincorporated association brings its organizing documents, such as bylaws or charter, and an EIN.

The Beneficial Ownership Rule applies to entities filed with a Secretary of State, not to sole proprietorships. It requires identifying anyone who owns 25% or more of the business and one individual with significant control, with a government-issued ID for each.

Business loans additionally require two years in business and Texas operation. The Business Advisor line is (512) 421-7310.
